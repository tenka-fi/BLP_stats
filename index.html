The market

Total size of the tkUSD / Senior Vault system. Bigger system → more capital cycling through → positions recycle faster.

The return tkUSD earns on its own (NAV accretion). The LP keeps earning this "carry" the whole time it holds.

Exit route 1. How much you can redeem at NAV in the weekly settlement window, filled by net new subscriptions and the liquidity sleeve. Sells your position back at par.

Exit route 2. Buy-side demand from other Backstop LPs in the pre-window. Lets you sell out early, usually at a small residual discount (set below).

Entry supply. Discounted paper available for you to buy each week. If this is thin, you can't stay fully deployed (see utilisation).

The trade

How far below fair value (NAV) the LP buys. 2% = paying $98 for $100 of exposure.

Tenka's liquidity fee, taken as a share of the discount. At 10%, the LP keeps ~90% of the discount.

Discount the LP gives up when it sells. 0% = exits at NAV (par redemption). Higher = it has to resell cheaply, keeping less of the gain.

How often you complete a round trip: buy below NAV, hold, sell back at NAV, repeat. Faster recycling compounds the discount more times per year, if the market can supply and absorb it.

Your position & risk

How much the Backstop LP puts to work. If this is large versus how fast the system recycles, positions take longer to turn and the annualised return falls.

What un-deployed capital earns while waiting for cheap paper (e.g. a money-market rate). Sets the floor when discounts are scarce.

Expected credit loss per year (NAV-drawdown risk). Applied over the holding period, so a short hold takes only a small slice. Senior ABF is typically a fraction of a percent a year.

Where the return comes from

Two parts: the discount captured at entry, and the tkUSD carry earned while holding. The faster the LP can recycle, the more times per year that stacks up.

What limits it

Two sides: discounted paper to buy (entry), and room to exit, either redeeming at NAV in settlement or selling to other Backstop LPs. Throughput is the smaller of the two; idle capital earns the base rate.

Simple, not compounding

Profits are counted but not reinvested into a larger next trade, because system recycle capacity is finite. Same principal turns over.

netDiscount     = discount × (1 − spreadFee)
carry(h)        = tkUSDyield × h/52            ( h = your chosen recycle cadence, weeks )
lossHaircut     = annualLoss × h/52
cycleROI        = ( (1+carry)·(1−exitDiscount)·(1−lossHaircut) − (1−netDiscount) ) / (1−netDiscount)
exitCapacity    = weeklyReplenish + NAV redemption + secondary bid
throughput      = min(exitCapacity, weeklyAsk)     ( need a seller to buy from AND a buyer to sell to )
utilisation     = min(1, throughput / (LPcapital / h))
returnAtCadence = cycleROI × 52/h
expectedNet     = utilisation × returnAtCadence + (1 − utilisation) × idleRate
What this does not include: gas and operational costs, competition compressing discounts over time, correlated stress (many facilities weakening at once), gate/redemption limits during stress, and reinvestment of profits. Annualised figures assume discounted supply keeps appearing; the utilisation term dampens this but does not model episodic droughts. Illustrative, not a projection of realised returns.
